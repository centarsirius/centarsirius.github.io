Sachin VenkateshAbout

I'm Sachin Venkatesh, a Physics PhD candidate at Northwestern University, working broadly on physics-informed ML+AI advised by Prof. Alexander Tchekhovskoy. I was previously doing my PhD at Georgia Tech where I received my MS in Computational Science and Engineering and MS in Physics. Prior to that, I received my B.Tech in Engineering Physics from Delhi Technological University.

My research primarily focuses on physics-informed neural-operator emulators for 3D GRMHD black-hole simulations — forecasting full simulation states on the native grid as boundary conditions for a hybrid simulation+emulation framework. Previously, I worked on unsupervised GRB classification at NAOJ Japan, IllustrisTNG at CCA Flatiron, turbulence super-resolution at DTU, and production data engineering (Snowflake/SQL/PySpark) at Genpact.

Core stack spans PyTorch/TensorFlow, JAX, CUDA, MPI, and multi-node accelerated training — emphasizing physical validity like conservation laws and statistical rigor over pure pixel error.

I'm always happy to talk and learn more about physics-informed ML, GRMHD astrophysics, or time-domain ML — please reach out below.

Research spotlightRESEARCH

My simulations vs. my emulator — drag the slider: left = H-AMR ground truth, right = neural operator forecast

Hybrid emulator for 3D GRMHD simulations — in prep.; follow-up simulation+emulation in prep.

Multiple snapshots (cadence Δt = 25 Rg/c), 8 primitives from a 3D GRMHD simulation + full Kerr metric. Factorized angular+radial convs, Fourier operator bottleneck, 9-objective physics loss with unrolled training. Stable 1/10/50-step rollouts preserving mass profiles, physical diagnostics, and jet morphology for hybrid simulation+emulation boundary conditions.

Stability across horizons. Same target frame forecast from 10 → 100 earlier snapshots (250 → 2500 Rg/c): disk structure and jet funnel persist even as the exact turbulent realization diverges — the statistical fidelity a boundary-condition emulator needs.
